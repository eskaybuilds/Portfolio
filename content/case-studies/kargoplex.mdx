## The problem

Kargoplex is a freight platform. Shippers request and book shipments, and freight forwarders, who are Kargoplex's clients, provide the shipping services. Each side has its own mobile app: the Shipper app and the Forwarder app.

That meant two apps to keep consistent, both handling payments, and both expected to keep working when the connection drops.

## My role

I was the only engineer on the mobile team. I worked across both apps, which are bare React Native, and I submitted and released both to the app stores.

## What I built

- Shipment tracking, booking and status updates, using Google Maps and location APIs, across both apps.
- Payment gateway integration with idempotency safeguards against duplicate transactions.
- Offline caching and synchronisation with TanStack Query, Firebase push notifications, and deep links.
- Reusable adapter-based components, and complex forms with React Hook Form and Yup.

## Decisions and trade-offs

**Shipment updates work offline.** A user can update a shipment with no connection. The change is kept on the device and synced when the connection comes back.

**TanStack Query for server state.** I introduced TanStack Query to the apps after seeing how well it handles API state. It also gave me the caching that the offline behaviour is built on.

**The app creates the idempotency key.** The failures I was guarding against were double taps and retries. The app generates an idempotency key for each payment, so a second tap or a retry carries the same key and cannot become a second transaction.

**Adapters between shared logic and native components.** I separated the reusable component logic from the native components underneath it. Switching a library or a provider then meant changing one place, not hundreds of lines across two apps. It also kept components standard across the Shipper and Forwarder apps.

## Outcome

Both apps were submitted and released to the app stores.
